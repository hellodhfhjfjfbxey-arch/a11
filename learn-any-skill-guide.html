How to Learn Any Skill

Whether it is pottery, coding or the guitar, learning a new skill follows the same simple pattern. Here is how to get started and keep going.

  1. 1Choose one skill and a reason
  2. 2Break it into small parts
  3. 3Learn just enough to start
  4. 4Practise deliberately
  5. 5Get feedback and make things
0 of 5 lessons done
Lesson 1

Choose one skill and a reason

Pick a single skill to focus on for the next month or two. Write down why it matters to you: a reason you care about will carry you through the frustrating early weeks far better than a vague wish.

Lesson 2

Break it into small parts

Most skills are a bundle of smaller sub-skills. Break yours down and choose the few that matter most at the start.

  • Guitar: a handful of chords, changing between them, a simple strum.
  • Coding: variables, loops, functions, reading error messages.
  • Pottery: wedging, centring, opening, pulling up a wall.
Lesson 3

Learn just enough to start

Gather two or three good resources, such as one book, one course or class and one community, then stop researching. Learn enough to practise and correct yourself, and come back to theory when you get stuck.

Lesson 4

Practise deliberately

Short, focused sessions on the hardest part beat long, comfortable ones. Work just beyond what you can do easily, slow down when you make mistakes, and repeat the tricky bit rather than the whole piece.

Lesson 5

Get feedback and make things

Record yourself, share your work, take a class or find a practice partner. Feedback shows you what to fix next. Aim for small, finished projects early: a bowl, a song, a tiny app. Finishing builds confidence and skill together.
